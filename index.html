AudioLift: Audio-Driven Single Image to
Full-Head Gaussian Avatar via Multi-view Video Generation

Zhuoyuan Jiang1, Weijie Lyu2, Xiaoyuan Wang3, Tiancheng Shen2, Ming-Hsuan Yang2
1Yale University, 2University of California, Merced, 3Carnegie Mellon University
Paper arXiv Video Code

AudioLift turns a single portrait and an audio clip into a full-head 3D Gaussian talking avatar, with no parametric face model and no per-instance optimization. Generated multi-view videos are the intermediate representation: an audio-conditioned video model synthesizes synchronized views, and an inconsistency-robust Gaussian reconstructor lifts every frame to 3D, so the avatar can be rendered at any time from any viewpoint across the full 360° range, keeping extreme expressions and facial accessories.

Abstract

Existing 3D talking avatar methods mainly rely on parametric face models including 3DMM or FLAME, whose compact motion representations limit the models' ability to synthesize extreme expressions and to model facial accessories. We introduce AudioLift, a two-stage framework that uses generated multi-view videos as the intermediate representation for audio-driven avatar generation without parametric face models or per-instance optimization. Our framework combines a synchronized multi-view video generation stage with an inconsistency-robust Gaussian reconstruction stage to recover coherent full-head geometry from a single portrait image. Extensive experiments demonstrate that AudioLift produces 3D talking avatars capable of capturing more extreme facial expressions than existing methods, with improved geometric completeness and strong multi-view consistency across the full 360° viewing range.

Method

Two-Stage Pipeline

Stage I: audio-driven multi-view generation. Five synchronized videos are generated from a single portrait and speech. The clean reference view conditions the denoiser, and the side and frontal views are interleaved into one token sequence where shared self-attention couples the views and promotes cross-view consistency.
Stage II: 3D Gaussian sequence reconstruction. A fine-tuned feed-forward reconstructor lifts the generated frames at each timestamp to a 3D Gaussian avatar. It is trained with view-aware corruption and active-volume regularization to tolerate inconsistent views, and completes unseen rear regions through conservative rear-prior distillation. The resulting Gaussian sequence can be rendered from any viewpoint at any time.

Results

Free-Viewpoint Talking Avatars from One Portrait

In-the-wild portraits driven by real recordings at their natural length. For every subject the same Gaussian avatar sequence is rendered from the front and from a moving camera: a full turn around the head, or a sweep across the viewing range the training cameras cover. Videos autoplay muted; Audio unmutes one subject at a time.
